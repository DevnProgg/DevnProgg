Designing a Basel III–Aligned Regulatory Calculation Engine (Spring Boot)

View on GitHub

I recently built a regulatory calculation engine designed to align with Basel III Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 expectations, focusing not just on correct capital calculations, but on governance, auditability, and reproducibility.

The Core Idea

In banking, capital numbers aren’t enough. Regulators expect:

  • Reproducible reporting by snapshot date
  • Deterministic calculations (no double counting)
  • Full data lineage from loan → RWA → capital
  • Controlled, auditable execution

So I designed the engine around those principles.

Architecture Highlights

Snapshot-Driven Design

Each run is tied to a regulatory reporting date. Results can be reproduced exactly — critical for supervisory review.

Idempotent Batch Processing (Spring Batch)

Re-running the same job produces the same outcome. This prevents financial misstatement risk.

Quartz-Scheduled Jobs

Automated, controlled reporting cycles reduce operational risk and support governance standards.

Basel-Aligned Calculation Types

Supports baseline and stress scenarios for ICAAP-style internal capital assessments.

Full Traceability

From exposure-level data to capital ratios — every number can be explained.

Why This Matters

In jurisdictions supervised under Basel III frameworks — including environments overseen by institutions such as the Central Bank of Lesotho — regulatory systems must demonstrate:

  • Strong internal controls
  • Model governance
  • Transparent calculation logic
  • Reproducibility for audit

This project was built with that standard in mind.
